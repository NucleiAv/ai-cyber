MITRE D3FEND, the defense map

A knowledge graph of defensive techniques, linked to the things they protect and to the ATT&CK techniques they counter.

The idea in one minute

ATT&CK describes what attackers do. D3FEND describes what defenders can do about it. MITRE builds it with funding from the US National Security Agency, at d3fend.mitre.org. The current version is 1.6.0.

Its key idea is the digital artifact. A file, a process, a network connection or a user account is an artifact. Attacks act on artifacts. Defenses watch or protect artifacts. So the artifact connects an attack to the defenses that fit it.

Think of it like this

If ATT&CK is a list of ways burglars get in, D3FEND is the hardware store catalog. Both mention "window". So when you read "burglar breaks a window", you can look up every product that protects a window.

Words you need

Defensive technique
A specific defense, such as Multi-factor Authentication or Network Traffic Analysis.
Digital artifact
A thing in a system that attackers and defenders both act on, such as a file, a process or a network flow.
Knowledge graph
Data stored as things and the links between them, so you can follow a path from an attack to a defense.
Tactic
A family of defenses. D3FEND has seven.

How it works, step by step

Seven defensive tactics

  1. Model. Know what you have, such as asset and network inventories.
  2. Harden. Make attacks harder before they start, such as multi-factor authentication or application hardening.
  3. Detect. Notice attacks, such as network traffic analysis or file analysis.
  4. Isolate. Limit where an attacker can go, such as network segmentation.
  5. Deceive. Mislead the attacker, such as decoy files or honeytokens.
  6. Evict. Remove the attacker, such as ending a process or locking an account.
  7. Restore. Return to a good state, such as restoring from backup.

From attack to defense in three hops

  1. Start with an ATT&CK technique, such as phishing.
  2. Find the artifacts it acts on, such as an email message and a URL.
  3. List the D3FEND techniques that act on the same artifacts, such as message analysis or URL analysis.

How to use it, step by step

  1. List the top ATT&CK techniques that worry you.
  2. For each, look up its artifacts in D3FEND.
  3. List the D3FEND techniques that cover those artifacts, across all seven tactics.
  4. Mark which you already run. Plan the rest, starting with Harden and Detect.
  5. Remember Restore. A defense plan that cannot recover is incomplete.

Real examples

The deepfake wire fraud in Module 8

The attack acts on a payment request. A process defense, a callback on a known number, hardens that artifact without trying to detect the fake.

Ransomware in Module 4 Case B

Detect found it, but nobody acted. Isolate and Evict were missing, and Restore decided how long recovery took.

Strengths and limits

Strengths

  • Precise names for defenses, so teams stop arguing about vague product claims.
  • Links to ATT&CK show which defense addresses which attack.
  • Free and machine readable.

Limits

  • It describes techniques, not products or settings. You still choose and configure tools.
  • Coverage of AI-specific defenses is still small compared with classic IT.

Try it

Pick one attack and find its defenses.

  1. Open d3fend.mitre.org.
  2. Search for an ATT&CK technique you met in this course, such as phishing.
  3. Write down two artifacts it acts on.
  4. List one D3FEND technique for at least four of the seven tactics.
  5. Circle the one you would fund first and say why.
