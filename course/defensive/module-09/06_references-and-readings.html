Recommended Readings

  • UK NCSC. "Prompt injection is not SQL injection (it may be worse)." December 8, 2025. Government
    Focus on the architectural argument: why parameterized queries cannot be replicated for natural language. Note the strength of the claim: "may never be properly mitigated." ~10 min.
    ncsc.gov.uk/blog-post/prompt-injection-is-not-sql-injection
  • OWASP Foundation. "OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications, Version 2025." November 2025. Framework
    Read the LLM01 (Prompt Injection) entry thoroughly. For the remaining nine categories, read the title and one-paragraph description. Note which categories relate to injection: LLM02, LLM05, LLM06, LLM07. ~15 min.
    genai.owasp.org/llm-top-10/
    On the numbering. OWASP revises this list periodically and the identifiers are not stable across editions: entries change position, merge, or get renamed. Every OWASP citation in this course is the 2025 edition, so the numbers stay consistent from Module 9 through Module 11. Treat the set of failure modes as the durable content and the numbering as a citation detail, and name the edition whenever you quote an identifier in professional work.
  • Greshake, K., Abdelnabi, S., Mishra, S., Endres, C., Holz, T., & Fritz, M. (2023). "Not what you've signed up for: Compromising Real-World LLM-Integrated Applications with Indirect Prompt Injection." AISec '23, ACM. Paper
    Read Sections 1 (introduction and threat model), 2-3 (attack taxonomy), and 6 (discussion). The key insight is the separation of attacker and user. Compare to the EchoLeak incident in Section 4. Published 2023; some demonstrations may have been mitigated, but the taxonomy remains current. ~30 min.
    arxiv.org/abs/2302.12173
  • Cohen, S., Bitton, R., & Nassi, B. (2024). "ComPromptMized: Unleashing Zero-click Worms that Target GenAI-Powered Applications" ("Morris II"). Preprint. Preprint
    Demonstrates a self-replicating prompt that spreads between AI email assistants, the lateral-movement / "worming" stage referenced in Section 5's kill chain. Research demonstration; treat as proof-of-concept. ~20 min.
    arxiv.org/abs/2403.02817
  • Zou, A., Wang, Z., Carlini, N., Nasr, M., Kolter, J. Z., & Fredrikson, M. (2023). "Universal and Transferable Adversarial Attacks on Aligned Language Models." Preprint. Preprint
    Introduces the GCG method: an automated greedy and gradient search finds adversarial suffixes, often not human-readable, that flip a model from refusing to complying and transfer across models from different vendors. Referenced in Section 1 as evidence that the instruction/data gap is architectural, not a wording problem. ~25 min.
    arxiv.org/abs/2307.15043
  • Anthropic. "Many-shot jailbreaking." April 2024. Practitioner
    First-party disclosure: a model provider demonstrates how filling the context window with hundreds of fabricated harmful Q&A pairs exploits in-context learning to override safety alignment. Effectiveness follows a power law over context length. Key reading for Section 3's jailbreaking coverage. ~15 min.
    anthropic.com/research/many-shot-jailbreaking

Further Optional Readings

  • Anthropic. "Many-shot jailbreaking." April 2024. Practitioner
    First-party disclosure of a vulnerability by a model provider. Demonstrates how in-context learning becomes a jailbreaking vector. ~15 min.
    anthropic.com/research/many-shot-jailbreaking
  • Schulhoff, S., et al. (2023). "Ignore This Title and HackAPrompt." EMNLP 2023 (Best Theme Paper). Paper
    Largest prompt injection dataset (600K+ adversarial prompts, 29 technique categories). Directly relevant to the CTF experience. ~25 min.
    arxiv.org/abs/2311.16119
  • Pleger, J., et al. (2026). "The Promptware Kill Chain." arXiv 2601.09625. Preprint
    Seven-stage attack chain framework. Documents 21 real incidents. Not peer-reviewed as of mid-2026. ~30 min.
    arxiv.org/abs/2601.09625
  • NIST. "Adversarial Machine Learning: A Taxonomy and Terminology of Attacks and Mitigations (AI 100-2e2025)." March 2025. Government
    Formal government taxonomy classifying prompt injection within the broader adversarial ML landscape. Read the prompt injection sections only. ~20 min.
    csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ai/100/2/e2025/final
  • Chen, Y., et al. (2025). "ASIDE: Architectural Separation of Instructions and Data in Language Models." ICLR 2025. Paper
    Proposes an architectural solution to the instruction/data boundary problem via orthogonal embedding rotation. ~25 min.
    arxiv.org/abs/2503.10566
  • Szekeres, L., Payer, M., Wei, T., & Song, D. (2013). "SoK: Eternal War in Memory." IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy (S&P). Paper
    Peer-reviewed systematization of memory-corruption defenses. Background for Section 1's hardware angle: it explains W^X / DEP, the NX bit, and why enforcing a code-versus-data boundary in hardware is what stops classical code injection, the very guarantee a transformer cannot provide. ~30 min.
    nebelwelt.net/teaching/18-725-LSS/01-EternalWar.pdf
  • Aim Security. "EchoLeak." arXiv 2509.10540. September 2025. Preprint
    Full technical write-up of the EchoLeak incident (CVE-2025-32711). Aim Security is a vendor; treat capability claims accordingly. ~20 min.
    arxiv.org/abs/2509.10540
  • RAND Corporation. Securing AI Model Weights: Preventing Theft and Misuse of Frontier Models by State Actors. 2024. Policy
    Analysis of the security challenges around protecting large model weights from exfiltration by state-level adversaries. Relevant to Section 1: GCG-style adversarial suffix attacks require access to model weights, making weight security a prerequisite for preventing gradient-based attacks on frontier models. ~30 min.
    rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA2849-1.html

Case Studies & Incident Reports

  • Chevrolet chatbot incident (December 2023). Incident
    ChatGPT-powered dealership chatbot manipulated via instruction override to agree to sell a vehicle for $1. AI Incident Database entry #622. Referenced in Sections 2 and 4.
    incidentdatabase.ai/cite/622/
  • EchoLeak / CVE-2025-32711 (June 2025). Incident
    Zero-click indirect prompt injection in Microsoft 365 Copilot (CVSS 9.3). Full write-up: Aim Security, arXiv 2509.10540. CVE record; Microsoft security advisory. Referenced in Section 4.
    arxiv.org/abs/2509.10540
  • Google Gemini memory poisoning (February 2025). Incident
    Delayed tool invocation used to inject persistent false memories into Gemini Advanced. Researcher disclosure (Johann Rehberger); Google acknowledgment. Referenced in Section 4.
  • GitHub Copilot markdown attack (2025). Incident
    Five crafted documents with invisible Markdown comments manipulated Copilot responses 90% of the time. Referenced in Section 4.

Source Caveats

Reading Sources Critically

Every source has a context. When reading security research, keep these caveats in mind:

Source Category Key Caveat
Model provider disclosures (Anthropic many-shot) First-party vulnerability disclosures are valuable but may understate the severity or scope relative to independent findings
Vendor security research (Aim Security / EchoLeak) Aim Security sells AI security products; treat capability claims and severity framing with vendor-source caution
Preprints (Promptware Kill Chain, arXiv 2601.09625) Not peer-reviewed as of mid-2026; treat incident counts and specific statistics as indicative, not definitive
Government guidance (UK NCSC, NIST) Authoritative for policy context but may not reflect the latest empirical findings
Benchmark results (HackAPrompt, jailbreak success rates) Benchmark performance depends on model version, prompt format, and evaluation criteria. Results from one model version may not transfer to later versions
CTF platforms (Gandalf, Prompt Airlines) Intentionally vulnerable by design; success rates and techniques do not directly predict production-system vulnerability
