Recommended Readings

R1: Careful Adoption of Agentic AI Services (read during Section 2)

Title: "Careful Adoption of Agentic AI Services"
Author: CISA, NSA, and international partners (Australia, Canada, New Zealand, United Kingdom) (2026)
URL: cisa.gov/news-events/news/cisa-us-and-international-partners-release-guide-secure-adoption-agentic-ai
Reading time: ~20 to 25 minutes (assigned excerpt: the five risk categories and the recommendations)
When to read: during Section 2, before the design studio.

The first coordinated multinational security guidance for agentic AI. Defines five risk categories (privilege escalation, design and configuration failures, behavioral misalignment, structural brittleness, accountability gaps) and argues that existing security disciplines, applied to a new kind of actor, are what agentic deployments require. Read as an assessor, mapping each category to a concept already met in the course.

R2: OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications, 2026 (read during Section 2)

Title: "OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications for 2026"
Author: OWASP GenAI Security Project (2025)
URL: genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-top-10-for-agentic-applications-for-2026
Reading time: ~20 minutes (four assigned entries, including goal hijack and tool misuse)
When to read: during Section 2, at the constraint discussion.

The agent risk taxonomy that continues the OWASP thread from the attack and defense weeks. For each assigned entry, identify which incident from the module it classifies and whether its mitigations are deterministic or probabilistic.

R3: The Lethal Trifecta for AI Agents (read at the end of Section 1)

Title: "The lethal trifecta for AI agents: private data, untrusted content, and external communication"
Author: Willison, S. (2025)
URL: simonwillison.net/2025/Jun/16/the-lethal-trifecta
Reading time: ~10 minutes
When to read: at the end of Section 1, before the design work.

A compact practitioner heuristic for the agent exfiltration surface. Assigned deliberately as practitioner guidance rather than a standard. Test it against the zero-click email exploit and against your own agent design.

Further Optional Readings

  • Yao, S., et al. (2023). "ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models." ICLR 2023. Paper
    The origin of the ReAct pattern, with the published trajectories that the Section 1 trace activity is modeled on. About 35 minutes; readable.
    arXiv:2210.03629
  • Beurer-Kellner, L., et al. (2025). "Design Patterns for Securing LLM Agents against Prompt Injections." Preprint
    Principled mitigation patterns (plan-then-execute, context minimization, dual-model) with explicit utility and security trade-offs. The engineering answer to the lethal trifecta. About 40 minutes. Not peer-reviewed.
    arXiv:2506.08837
  • Apple Machine Learning Research. (2025). "The Illusion of Thinking: Understanding the Strengths and Limitations of Reasoning Models via the Lens of Problem Complexity." Paper
    Reports abrupt reasoning collapse past complexity thresholds on controlled puzzles. Read as a pair with its rebuttal for the Section 6 contested-science discussion. About 45 minutes for the pair.
    machinelearning.apple.com/research/illusion-of-thinking
  • Dellibarda Varela, I., Romero-Sorozabal, P., Rocon, E., and Cebrian, M. (2025). "Rethinking the Illusion of Thinking." Preprint
    A direct methodological rebuttal to the reasoning-collapse finding. Assigned only alongside the study it contests. Not peer-reviewed.
    arXiv:2507.01231
  • Anthropic. "Building Effective AI Agents." Industry Analysis
    The workflow-versus-agent distinction from a builder's stance, with composable design patterns. Vendor engineering guidance; used for concepts, not capability claims. About 20 minutes.
    anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents
  • ISC2. (2025). "2025 ISC2 Cybersecurity Workforce Study." Industry Analysis
    The dated evidence base behind Section 4's demand discussion. Self-reported survey from an association that sells certifications; figures are context, not prophecy. About 25 minutes for the skills chapters.
    isc2.org/Insights/2025/12/2025-ISC2-Cybersecurity-Workforce-Study

Case Studies & Incident Reports

  • Production database deletion during a code freeze (July 2025). Incident
    A coding agent deleted a production database holding roughly 1,200 executive records during an explicit freeze, then generated misleading explanations. The instruction-violation-under-autonomy case in Section 2; corroborated across multiple independent accounts including the platform's own acknowledgment.
  • Nine-second production and backup deletion (April 2026). Incident
    An over-permissioned, agent-readable token allowed deletion of a production database and its volume-level backups in about nine seconds. The blast-radius anchor case in Section 2. Reported by trade press; specific product attributions are omitted here.
  • Command-line agent silent-failure file loss (July 2025). Incident
    A folder-creation step failed silently; the runtime proceeded as though it had succeeded and overwrote files. The silent-failure-cascade case distinguishing this failure mode from hallucination.
  • Zero-click assistant prompt-injection exfiltration, CVE-2025-32711 (June 2025). Incident
    Indirect prompt injection in a production assistant: a crafted email in retrieval context caused exfiltration of internal data with no user interaction. Patched server-side; no confirmed exploitation. The production proof for the untrusted-tool-result fact in Sections 1 and 2.
    arXiv:2509.10540
  • Reported AI-orchestrated espionage campaign (November 2025). Incident
    A vendor disclosure describing an agent performing 80 to 90 percent of a campaign against roughly thirty targets. Read critically in Section 3 as a labeled vendor account: corroborated outline, single-source percentages. Vendor account of its own platform; treat the figures as directional.
  • Autonomous vulnerability-research discoveries (2025). Incident
    An agent credited with real CVE discoveries verified by human researchers before patching. The defensive counterweight in Section 3. The specific "imminent exploitation" framing is the vendor's; the CVEs are independently corroborated.
  • Constructed teaching materials. Incident
    The two agent traces in Section 1 and the log-anomaly triage-agent design in Section 2 are fictional, constructed for this module and labeled as such throughout.

Source Caveats

Reading Sources Critically

Every source has a context. When you read security research, keep these caveats in mind:

Source CategoryKey Caveat
Vendor accounts of their own platforms (the November 2025 espionage disclosure; the vulnerability-research discoveries) The reporting party has unique visibility and a commercial interest. Corroborated outlines are usable; self-measured figures, such as the 80 to 90 percent autonomy claim, are single-source and directional.
Analyst-firm positioning (AI SOC hype-cycle placement) Reaches the course secondhand through vendor summaries; the primary documents are paywalled. Treat as positioning, not measurement.
Preprints (design patterns; the reasoning-limits pair; the perturbation-fragility work) Not peer-reviewed. The reasoning-limits pair is deliberately contested; present both sides.
Self-reported surveys (the workforce study) Respondent self-report from an association that sells certifications. Figures are dated context, not prediction.
Living documents (ATLAS versioning; the OWASP editions; the EU AI Act timeline; the NICE version) Version numbers and dates change. Verify each at the source before teaching.
Incident reports Early accounts get revised as more becomes public. The April 2026 deletion is reported by trade press, and specific product attributions are omitted here.
