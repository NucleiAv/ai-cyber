Academic Papers & Preprints

  • Carlini, N., Tramèr, F., Wallace, E., Jagielski, M., Herbert-Voss, A., Lee, K., Roberts, A., Brown, T., Song, D., Erlingsson, Ú., Oprea, A., & Raffel, C. (2021). "Extracting Training Data from Large Language Models." 30th USENIX Security Symposium, pp. 2633–2650. Paper
    Demonstrated that training data can be recovered verbatim from a deployed language model. The empirical basis for the memorization risk discussed in Section 2 and for treating fine-tuning data as a disclosure surface.
    USENIX Security '21 (PDF) · arXiv:2012.07805
  • Liu, N. F., Lin, K., Hewitt, J., Paranjape, A., Bevilacqua, M., Petroni, F., & Liang, P. (2023). "Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts." Transactions of the Association for Computational Linguistics. Paper
    Documents the retrieval degradation for information placed in the centre of a long input. The basis for the Section 1 knowledge check on the 40-page report.
    arXiv:2307.03172

Frameworks & Standards

  • OWASP GenAI Security Project. OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications. Framework
    The industry-recognized classification for LLM security risks, used in Section 3 to organize risks by lifecycle stage and revisited in depth in Modules 9 through 11.
    2026 edition (current) · 2025 edition · Project home
  • National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST). SP 800-218: Secure Software Development Framework (SSDF) v1.1. NIST, 2022. Framework
    Referenced in Section 3 as an example of a publication whose number and content students should verify rather than accept from model output.
    NIST SSDF project · SP 800-218 (PDF)
  • National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST). SP 800-218A: Secure Software Development Practices for Generative AI and Dual-Use Foundation Models. NIST. Framework
    An SSDF community profile that adds AI-specific practices across the model development lifecycle. The closest standards analogue to the lifecycle risk map built in Section 3.
    NIST SP 800-218A
  • MITRE. ATT&CK Knowledge Base. MITRE Corporation. Framework
    Referenced across Sections 2 and 3 as the adversary tactic and technique taxonomy that security teams already use, and as the model for how a shared vocabulary makes risks comparable.
    MITRE ATT&CK

Source Caveats

Reading Sources Critically

Every source has a context. When you read security research, keep these caveats in mind:

Source CategoryKey Caveat
Peer-reviewed papers (Carlini et al.; Liu et al.) Findings are tied to the specific model versions and context lengths tested. The “lost in the middle” effect in particular varies with architecture and context window, so treat the shape of the result as the lesson rather than the exact numbers.
Living frameworks (OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications, MITRE ATT&CK) Both are revised on their own schedules, and OWASP renumbers and reorders entries between editions. Cite the edition and the date you consulted it.
Regulation (EU AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) Obligations phase in across several years and implementing acts and standards continue to appear. Check the current consolidated text before relying on any specific duty or deadline.
Practice catalogs (NIST SP 800-218 and 800-218A) The SSDF is a catalog of practices to select from, not a compliance checklist and not a maturity score. It tells you what to consider, not what is sufficient.
Course-built interactives (tokenizer, pipeline stages, embedding explorer) Deliberately simplified teaching models. They reproduce the shape of the mechanism, not the arithmetic of any production system, and are not a source of measurements.
