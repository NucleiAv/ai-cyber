Recommended Readings

R1: NIST AI RMF, Part 2: The Core (read during Section 1)

Title: Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0), Part 2
Author: NIST (January 2023)
URL: nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf
Reading time: ~30 minutes
When to read: During Section 1, before the worked Map example.

The module's backbone: the four functions (Govern, Map, Measure, Manage) and their categories. Read for the shape of each function, holding one question open: "for the triage pipeline, what evidence would show this function is present?" Skip the appendices.

R2: Deploying AI Systems Securely (read between Sections 2 and 3)

Title: Deploying AI Systems Securely: Best Practices for Deploying Secure and Resilient AI Systems
Author: NSA AISC, CISA, FBI, and international partners (April 2024)
URL: cisa.gov/ai/cisa-products
Reading time: ~15 minutes
When to read: Between Sections 2 and 3, before the policy-drafting exercise.

Joint operational guidance for organizations deploying AI systems. Read as a policy writer: mark every practice that implies an organizational rule (who approves, who logs, who reviews), then convert three of them into testable statements in the drafting exercise.

R3: EU AI Act, Article 14: Human Oversight (read during Section 4)

Title: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 14: Human Oversight
Author: European Union (2024)
URL: eur-lex.europa.eu (Regulation 2024/1689)
Reading time: ~10 minutes
When to read: During Section 4, before the classification reasoning task.

Primary regulatory text, one article, read slowly. Note which oversight expectations assume the human can actually dissent, and compare them against the automation-bias evidence from Section 2. A navigable unofficial mirror is listed under Datasets & Tools.

Further Optional Readings

  • NIST. (2024). AI RMF: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile (NIST AI 600-1). Framework
    Function-level treatment of generative-AI-specific risks; the executive summary was assigned earlier in the course. ~45 minutes. Verify the current edition before relying on section numbers.
    nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf
  • NIST NCCoE. (2025). Cybersecurity Framework Profile for Artificial Intelligence (Cyber AI Profile), NIST IR 8596, preliminary draft, December 2025. Framework
    The bridge between the AI RMF and the cybersecurity framework security teams already use; pairs with the profile-mapping exercise at the end of Section 4. ~40 minutes. Draft status; may be superseded.
    csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8596/iprd
  • Horowitz, M. C., & Kahn, L. (2024). "Bending the Automation Bias Curve: A Study of Human and AI-Based Decision Making in National Security Contexts." International Studies Quarterly, 68(2). Paper
    The nine-country, ~9,000-participant experiment behind Section 2's automation-bias material. ~40 minutes; open access.
    academic.oup.com/isq (sqae020)

    The origin of the dual-use publication-norms debate exercised in the Section 4 discussion; read the interventions section and note how little has settled since. ~30 minutes for the excerpt. Multi-institution report; not peer-reviewed.
    arXiv:1802.07228
  • NIST. (2019). Face Recognition Vendor Test (FRVT) Part 3: Demographic Effects (NISTIR 8280), executive summary. Government
    Primary source behind Section 2's face recognition case, and a model of careful disparity phrasing: magnitudes are algorithm- and image-quality-dependent. ~15 minutes.
    nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2019/nist.ir.8280.pdf
  • White House OSTP. (2025). Winning the Race: America's AI Action Plan, Pillar II security actions. Government
    Primary source for Section 4's US posture snapshot; read the security-relevant actions with the evidence-calibration lens from earlier in the course. ~25 minutes for the excerpt. Regulatory content; verify currency.
    whitehouse.gov (Americas-AI-Action-Plan.pdf)
  • Sap, M., et al. (2019). "The Risk of Racial Bias in Hate Speech Detection." ACL 2019. Paper
    The annotation-bias mechanism behind Section 2's dialect-bias case, in the original. ~30 minutes; open access.
    aclanthology.org/P19-1163

Academic Papers & Preprints

  • Horowitz, M. C., & Kahn, L. (2024). "Bending the Automation Bias Curve: A Study of Human and AI-Based Decision Making in National Security Contexts." International Studies Quarterly, 68(2). Paper
    Empirical grounding for Section 2's oversight failure modes: over-trust varies systematically with AI background knowledge; calibration failures persist among experienced participants.
    academic.oup.com/isq (sqae020)
  • Sap, M., Card, D., Gabriel, S., Choi, Y., & Smith, N. A. (2019). "The Risk of Racial Bias in Hate Speech Detection." ACL 2019. Paper
    Documented systematically higher false positive rates for African-American English in toxicity classifiers, traced to annotation practice. The labels-door case in Section 2's bias mechanism table.
    aclanthology.org/P19-1163
  • Garg, T., et al. (2023). "Handling Bias in Toxic Speech Detection: A Survey." ACM Computing Surveys. Paper
    Survey consolidating bias findings and mitigations across abuse-detection systems; background for Section 2's treatment.
    arXiv:2202.00126
  • Brundage, M., et al. (2018). The Malicious Use of Artificial Intelligence: Forecasting, Prevention, and Mitigation. Preprint
    Foundational dual-use framing and intervention taxonomy for Section 2's dual-use discussion and the Section 4 disclosure debate. Multi-institution report; not peer-reviewed.
    arXiv:1802.07228
  • NIST. (2019, 2022). FRVT Part 3: Demographic Effects (NISTIR 8280) and FRVT Part 8: Summarizing Demographic Differentials (NIST IR 8429). Government
    The 189-algorithm demographic evaluation behind Section 2's face recognition case: false positive differentials far exceeding false negative differentials, varying by algorithm.
    pages.nist.gov/frvt (nistir_8280)
  • "Evaluating the Robustness of LLM Guardrail Models." (2025). Preprint
    Tested 10 guardrail models across 1,445 prompts; best accuracy 85.3%, dropping to 33.8% on unseen prompts. Source for Section 1's bypass-rate opening, carried over from the defending-AI-systems module. Not peer-reviewed.
    arXiv:2511.22047
  • Kleinberg, J., Mullainathan, S., & Raghavan, M. (2017). "Inherent Trade-Offs in the Fair Determination of Risk Scores." ITCS 2017. Paper
    One of two independent proofs of the fairness-metric impossibility behind Section 2's "fix it to what?" discussion: common fairness criteria cannot be satisfied simultaneously when groups' underlying rates differ. Source for the Section 4 fairness exercise.
    arXiv:1609.05807
  • Chouldechova, A. (2017). "Fair Prediction with Disparate Impact: A Study of Bias in Recidivism Prediction Instruments." Big Data, 5(2), 153–163. Paper
    The other independent proof of the same impossibility result, arriving at it through calibration: a calibrated classifier cannot have equal false positive and false negative rates across groups unless the groups have the same base rate.
    arXiv:1703.00056

Case Studies & Incident Reports

  • Clearview AI enforcement arc (2022-2025). Incident
    Scraped biometric database for police identification; data protection fines across multiple European states and a UK penalty with jurisdiction affirmed in October 2025. Cumulative fine totals vary by counting method and appeal status; Section 2 states them as "tens of millions of euros" deliberately. Sources: national regulator decisions; UK ICO (above).
  • SyRI ruling and Dutch childcare benefits scandal (2020-2021). Incident
    Government algorithmic risk-scoring struck down under the European Convention on Human Rights, Article 8. The broader childcare benefits scandal saw thousands of families wrongly accused of fraud and ordered to repay benefits; the Dutch government resigned in January 2021. Section 2's proportionality anchor. Sources: The Hague District Court ruling and Amnesty International report (above).
  • NIST face recognition demographic evaluations (2019 onward). Incident
    Not an incident but an official evaluation used as a case: false positive differentials across demographic groups far exceeding false negative differentials. Source: NISTIR 8280 / IR 8429 (above).
  • Autonomous-agent database deletion (mid-2025). Incident
    Production database destroyed by an over-scoped agentic tool; no attacker involved. Analyzed in the adversarial-AI module as a design failure and re-analyzed in Section 1 here as a Govern/Map failure. Source: as documented in the Module 10 references.
  • Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024 BCCRT 149 (February 2024). Incident
    British Columbia Civil Resolution Tribunal decision holding an airline liable for bereavement-fare advice its website chatbot gave a customer. The airline argued the chatbot was "a separate legal entity that is responsible for its own actions"; the tribunal called this "a remarkable submission" and held that "it makes no difference whether the information comes from a static page or a chatbot." Source for Section 1's accountability case. Small-claims tribunal decision, not an appellate judgment; persuasive rather than binding outside British Columbia.
  • NorthShore Mutual (fictional). Primary Source
    The Section 3 and 4 scenario organization. Fictional composite built for this course from prior-module material; labeled as fictional at every appearance. No external source.

Source Caveats

Reading Sources Critically

Every source has a context. When you read security research, keep these caveats in mind:

Source Category Key Caveat
Regulatory texts and timelines (EU AI Act, US executive actions) All statements dated as of July 2026. The EU timeline was amended in June 2026 and US federal policy reversed in January 2025; verify current status before relying on any date in this module
NIST draft documents (IR 8596 Cyber AI Profile) Preliminary draft; content and structure may change before final publication
Court and regulator decisions (Clearview, SyRI) Outcomes stated with dates; appeal statuses evolve, and cumulative fine totals vary by counting method
Academic preprints (guardrail robustness evaluation, arXiv:2511.22047; the 2018 malicious-use report, arXiv:1802.07228) Not peer-reviewed; treat claims with appropriate caution. The malicious-use report is influential and widely cited but remains a report, not a reviewed study
Vendor certification claims (ISO/IEC 42001) Certification attests that a management process exists and was audited, not that any system is safe; treat as procurement evidence, not assurance
Incident databases (AI Incident Database, OECD Monitor) Definitions differ between databases; counts are volatile and press-dependent
