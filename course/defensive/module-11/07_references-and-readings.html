Recommended Readings

R1: OWASP LLM Top 10 (Selected Entries) (Before Section 5)

Title: "OWASP Top 10 for Large Language Model Applications (2025)"
Author: OWASP Foundation (2025)
URL: owasp.org/www-project-top-10-for-large-language-model-applications/
Reading time: ~20 minutes (selected entries: LLM01, LLM05, LLM06, LLM08)
When to read: Before beginning Section 5's OWASP defensive assessment.

Read the full entries for LLM01 (Prompt Injection), LLM05 (Improper Output Handling), LLM06 (Excessive Agency), and LLM08 (Vector and Embedding Weaknesses). Focus on the "Prevention and Mitigation Strategies" subsections, which provide the defensive checklist for the OWASP assessment activity.

On the numbering. OWASP revises this list periodically and the identifiers are not stable across editions: entries change position, merge, or get renamed. Every OWASP citation in this course is the 2025 edition, so the numbers stay consistent from Module 9 through Module 11. Treat the set of failure modes as the durable content and the numbering as a citation detail, and name the edition whenever you quote an identifier in professional work.

R2: NIST AI 600-1 Executive Summary (Before Section 1)

Title: "Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile" (NIST AI 600-1)
Author: NIST (2024)
URL: nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf
Reading time: ~15 minutes (executive summary and Information Security category)
When to read: Before beginning Section 1.

Focus on the executive summary and the Information Security risk category. The framework's 13 risk categories and Govern/Map/Measure/Manage structure provide context for the defense-in-depth approach covered in Section 1.

R3: CoSAI AI Incident Response Framework (Before Section 4)

Title: "AI Incident Response Framework, Version 1.0"
Author: Coalition for Secure AI (2026)
URL: coalitionforsecureai.org/AI-Incident-Response-1.pdf
Reading time: ~15 minutes (executive summary and architecture-specific playbook sections)
When to read: Before beginning Section 4.

The most comprehensive AI-specific incident response framework available. Includes architecture-specific playbooks in OASIS CACAO format. Section 4 builds on this framework's three key differences between AI and traditional incident response: probabilistic evidence, ephemeral state, and continuous harm.

Academic Papers & Preprints

  • Nasr, M., et al. (2025). "The Attacker Moves Second." Joint OpenAI/Anthropic/Google DeepMind study. Preprint
    All 12 tested prompt injection defenses bypassed at >90% success under adaptive attacks. Landmark negative result establishing that no single defense is sufficient. Source for Section 1's defense-in-depth justification. Not peer-reviewed.
  • Zou, W., et al. (2025). "PoisonedRAG: Knowledge Poisoning Attacks to Retrieval-Augmented Generation of Large Language Models." USENIX Security 2025. Paper
    Demonstrated 90-99% attack success by injecting 5 adversarial documents into a RAG knowledge base of 400 million. Foundational paper for Section 2's RAG security discussion and PoisonedRAG case analysis.
    arXiv:2402.07867
  • "Evaluating the Robustness of LLM Guardrail Models." (2025). Preprint
    Tested 10 guardrail models across 1,445 prompts. Best accuracy 85.3%, dropping to 33.8% on unseen prompts. Source for Section 1's guardrail effectiveness evidence. Not peer-reviewed.
    arXiv:2511.22047
  • "Pr∈∈mpt: Sanitizing Sensitive Prompts via Differential Privacy." NDSS 2026. Paper
    Differential-privacy-based prompt sanitization using format-preserving encryption. Emerging research direction referenced in Section 1.
    ndss-symposium.org/2026-s1277
  • "Attention Tracker: Detecting Prompt Injection via Attention Pattern Analysis." NAACL 2025 Findings. Paper
    AI-specific prompt injection detection mechanism using attention pattern analysis. Relevant to Section 3's monitoring discussion.
    aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.123
  • "Prompt Injection in Resume Screening: A Field Study." (2026). Preprint
    Approximately 1% of 83,000 real resumes contained hidden prompt injection over 17 months. Grounds the defense discussion in production data. Not peer-reviewed.
    arXiv:2605.28999
  • "Securing Retrieval-Augmented Generation: A Taxonomy." (2026). Preprint
    Comprehensive taxonomy of RAG security threats and defenses. Background reference for Section 2. Not peer-reviewed.
    arXiv:2604.08304
  • "Synthesizing Access Control Policies using Large Language Models." (2025). Preprint
    Tested zero-shot LLM generation of AWS IAM policies against 47 ground-truth policies using SMT solvers. Source for Section 6's IAM analysis discussion. Not peer-reviewed.
    arXiv:2503.11573
  • "TerraFormer: LLM-Based Terraform Generation and Repair with Automated Verification." ICSE-SEIP 2026. Paper
    LLM-based IaC generation and repair. Key finding: LLMs perform better on YAML/JSON than HCL formats. Referenced in Section 6.
    arXiv:2601.08734
  • "TerraProbe: A Five-Layer Oracle Framework for Evaluating LLM-Generated Terraform Security Repairs." (2026). Preprint
    20.4% of LLM-generated repairs that passed linter checks failed schema validation or missed the real issue. Source for Section 6's IaC limitation discussion. Not peer-reviewed.
    arXiv:2606.26590
  • "LLMs for Terraform Code Smell Detection." IEEE COMPSAC 2025. Paper
    LLMs outperformed static analysis at detecting logic-level code smells in Terraform configurations. Source for Section 6's IaC discussion.
    computer.org/csdl/compsac/2025
  • "AutoYara: Automated YARA Rule Generation." (2020). ACM AISec 2020 (Best Paper). Paper
    Automated YARA rule generation using biclustering. Reduced analyst time by 44-86%. Source for Section 6's YARA discussion.
    dl.acm.org/10.1145/3411508.3421372
  • Wang, et al. (2025). "LLMDYara: LLMs Driven Automated YARA." Black Hat USA 2025. Paper
    Extends AutoYara with LLM capabilities for more human-readable YARA rules based on behavioral descriptions. Conference presentation; not peer-reviewed.
    blackhat.com/BH-USA-25
  • "Malware Detection with LightGBM." (2025). Nature Scientific Reports. Paper
    97.16% accuracy with LightGBM using PCA dimensionality reduction and feature scaling for malware behavioral classification. Source for Section 6's malware classification discussion.
    nature.com/articles/s41598-025-93447-x
  • "An Empirical Study of LLM Deployment." (2025). Preprint
    Found 158 API endpoints across 15 LLM deployment frameworks, many with minimal or default authentication. Source for Section 2's API-exposed deployment pattern. Not peer-reviewed.
    arXiv:2505.02502

Source Caveats

Reading Sources Critically

Every source has a context. When you read security research, keep these caveats in mind:

Source CategoryKey Caveat
Preprints (arXiv papers not yet peer-reviewed) Several key findings in this module come from preprints: guardrail robustness evaluation (85% to 34% accuracy), IAM policy synthesis, TerraProbe IaC repair rates, and the resume prompt injection field study. Preprints have not undergone formal peer review. Treat findings as directional evidence, not established fact, until published at a peer-reviewed venue.
Vendor tool documentation (Meta, NVIDIA, Guardrails AI) Tool capabilities and performance metrics are self-reported by the vendor. The guardrail tool comparison in Section 1 lists vendor-stated features; independent benchmarks may show different results. Architecture types (rule-based, model-based, hybrid) are the durable knowledge; specific tool capabilities may change between versions.
Incident reports (denial-of-wallet, cross-tenant leakage) Incident details are based on published accounts that may not include full forensic analysis. Dollar figures for denial-of-wallet incidents ($46K/day, $82K/48hr) come from the reporting parties and have not been independently audited. The architectural lessons (token-based rate limiting, database-level isolation) hold regardless of exact figures.
Practitioner guides (Datadog, Microsoft, CSA) Practitioner sources reflect current operational practice, which may not be evidence-based. Vendor-published practitioner content (Datadog, Microsoft, AWS) may reflect commercial interests. Use for implementation guidance; verify claims against academic sources where possible.
