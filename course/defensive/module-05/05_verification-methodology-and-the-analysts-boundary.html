The Five Step Methodology

Goal

A repeatable methodology to turn "double check the AI" from a vague intention into professional practice. Where exactly does the analyst's non delegable work begin?

We have covered what intelligence is, what ground truth exists, how models accelerate the text work, and how structure reaches machines. What remains is to make verification systematic. Unsystematic verification decays under deadline pressure into spot checking, and that is precisely where operates. We have been building defenses against it since Module 2's verification workflow; this methodology names the habit and adds two steps requires. The methodology below is designed to carry across modules, we will apply it to code review findings and to training data in modules 6 and 7!

Steps 1 through 3 build directly on the verification workflow we practiced in Module 2 and the critical evaluation checklist from Module 3, applied here by name to CTI claim types. Steps 4 and 5 are what intelligence work specifically adds: time-sensitivity and signed confidence.

  1. 1

    Decompose into atomic claims

    Break composite model output into individual (one verifiable assertion each) so that one correct claim cannot vouch for the rest. (Module 2, Step 1.)

  2. 2

    Type each claim

    Classify the claim: existence, technical detail (versions, vectors, scores), exploitation status, mitigation, or attribution. The type determines the route. (Module 2, Step 2.)

  3. 3

    Route each claim to its authoritative source

    Use the source map we built in Section 2: those are the coordinates. As a reminder: existence claims route to cve.org; severity (CVSS score and vector) routes to NVD; exploitation status routes to the CISA KEV catalog; affected and fixed versions route to the vendor advisory. We are not researching from scratch; we are looking up a known address. References route to the cheapest check: click them.

  4. 4

    Check currency

    A claim can be true for the moment its source was written and false now. Fixed versions get superseded; mitigations get retracted; actors retool. Ask not only "was this ever true" but "is this still true," and date stamp what you verified. This step exists because of the knowledge cutoff: models serve the past with present tense confidence.

  5. 5

    Assign confidence and mark residue

    Grade what you verified and say so in the product. Claims you could not verify are marked unverified in the disseminated product, not silently retained because they are probably fine.

The methodology is cheaper than it looks

Decomposition and typing take a minute. Routing is fast because the source map gives us exactly one primary source per claim type; we are not researching from scratch, we are looking up known coordinates. The reference click test costs seconds. The expensive steps, currency and confidence, are expensive precisely where the claims matter most, which is the correct place for an analyst's time to pool. Verification of known claim types is a lookup, not research: the net saving of draft plus verify over write from scratch is real. What is eliminated is the fantasy of draft without verify.

Grading, Currency, and Confidence

Step 5 needs vocabulary, and two standard ones serve. The grades source reliability and information credibility separately; "B2" encodes both judgments in two characters. per maps probability to calibrated words: "likely," "almost certainly." Whatever scales your future employer uses, the transferable parts are two-axis grading and calibrated language (words with agreed probability ranges, not vibes).

The deserves its own emphasis because it catches a failure that existence and detail checks cannot. Recall the failure taxonomy from the summarization discussion: stale truth is not hallucination. The model accurately reports something that is no longer true. Log4Shell is the canonical case: 2.15.0 was the fix for a few days in December 2021, then was itself superseded because the initial fix was incomplete. A model trained during that window serves "upgrade to 2.15.0" forever, fluently, and every existence check passes.

Try it: watch an answer expire

Press Advance time and hold the model still while the world moves. Its recommendation, “upgrade to 2.15.0,” was correct the day it was trained and stays frozen there. As later releases supersede it, the existence check keeps passing while the currency check fails. That gap is stale truth, and only step 4 of the methodology catches it.

The idea of a model deliberately frozen at a past date is developed in Vintage Large Language Models, Owain Evans (2024) , talk transcript, with slides and video.

Worked Example: Six Claims, Five Steps

The model paragraph below is an illustrative reconstruction, labeled as such. The underlying vulnerability facts are real and checkable. A model, asked without grounding about Log4Shell remediation for a briefing, produces: 

// Reconstructed model output. Claim numbers added for the walkthrough.
"CVE-2021-44228 (Log4Shell) is a critical RCE in Apache
Log4j 2 (1) with CVSS 10.0. (2) It is actively exploited
in the wild. (3) Upgrading to Log4j 2.15.0 fully remediates
the issue. (4) The campaign exploiting it most aggressively
is attributed to a single state sponsored group. (5)
Organizations should patch within 24 hours." (6)

Steps 1 and 2 · Decompose and type

Six atomic claims: (1) existence and identity, (2) technical detail, (3) exploitation status, (4) mitigation, (5) attribution, (6) action recommendation.

Steps 3 and 4 · Route each claim and check currency

  1. 1

    Claims 1 to 3: verified

    The CVE record verifies existence and identity. NVD verifies CVSS 10.0 with the vector we read earlier. KEV verifies exploitation: historically true, and the entry exists. Stable under the currency check.
  2. 2

    Claim 4: contradicted as stated. The currency casualty

    The Apache advisory shows 2.15.0 was followed by further fixes (2.16.0 and later) because the initial fix was incomplete. The claim was approximately true for a few days in December 2021. This is the model serving stale truth fluently. The verified replacement (upgrade to the current fixed line per the vendor advisory) is what ships.
  3. 3

    Claim 5: unverifiable as stated, contradicted in spirit

    Primary reporting documented exploitation by many actors, criminal and state-linked. "A single state-sponsored group" earns a poor grade on the information credibility axis. claims are exactly where model output should be treated most skeptically: deep uncertainty and active deception are structural features of the problem, not artifacts of current model quality.
  4. 4

    Claim 6: not a verifiable claim at all

    "Patch within 24 hours" is a risk decision. Routing it anywhere misses the point: it gets flagged as a human judgment that depends on the organization's exposure, change windows, and risk acceptance. The model can propose it; only the organization can decide it.

Step 5, the disseminated artifact: a briefing in which claims 1 to 3 carry verified status with sources and dates, claim 4 is replaced with current guidance, claim 5 is removed or explicitly marked unsubstantiated, and claim 6 is converted into a recommendation block addressed to the risk owner. The product also states what was not verified. That marked up, signed artifact is the deliverable format for the verification lab.

Watch out: two misconceptions about verification

"Verification means redoing the research, so AI saves nothing." Routing known claim types to known sources is a lookup, not research. What is eliminated is draft without verify, not the time saving.

"Confidence comes from how sure the model sounds." Confidence is assigned by the analyst after verification, expressed in calibrated vocabulary, and owned by a person. The model's tone is evidence of nothing.

4

The Analyst's Boundary and TLP

The analyst's boundary is clearest when argued from properties rather than sentiment. Certain steps of intelligence work are irreducibly human not because models are currently weak at them, but because of what those steps fundamentally are:

1

Accountability

Module 4 established that consequential outputs require a human owner. In CTI, ownership has a specific form: a signed assessment with a named analyst, professional standing, and an institutional accountability chain behind it. A model cannot be questioned about its reasoning next quarter, cannot accept professional consequences, and cannot be subpoenaed. Where output becomes an organizational position, a human adopts it or it ships as nothing.

Ownership
2

Access

In Module 4's enrichment step, the model could not know whether a specific workstation or domain controller was in your environment. At the full-product level that gap scales: your asset inventory, internal telemetry, prior incidents, and sector context are all absent from any public model's training. The model reasons about the general case; the analyst reasons about your case.

Evidence
3

Irreversibility

Recommending emergency patching, taking systems offline, or attributing activity to a named actor in public are actions with costs that cannot be unspent. Decision authority over irreversible actions stays with humans as a matter of governance, not capability.

Governance
4

Attribution and intent

Who is behind activity and what they want are judgments under deep uncertainty, where deception is an active variable and the evidence is partly non textual. This is the canonical territory of structured analytic technique and human judgment.

Judgment

The boundary may move as models change; those four properties travel with it, because they are features of the work and the institution, not of current model quality.

The boundary has a governance counterpart in dissemination. The , standardized by as TLP 2.0, marks sharing constraints on intelligence across five levels from TLP:CLEAR (no restriction) to TLP:RED (named recipients only). Here is where it bites this course: pasting TLP:AMBER reporting into an external AI service transmits restricted intelligence to a third party. That is a sharing violation regardless of how useful the summary would be, and it is one of the concrete reasons this course runs models locally: Module 2's deployment lesson, now with a compliance face. TLP binds by agreement and professional norm, applies to unclassified intelligence cooperation, and is not a government classification system.

Your reasoning task: boundary mapping with justification

An eight step weekly workflow is listed below. Mark each step AI delegable, AI drafted with human verification, or human only. Justify every human-only call by naming which property forces it (accountability, access, irreversibility, attribution or judgment under uncertainty, or a TLP constraint), not by intuition. More than one mapping is defensible for steps 3, 4, and 6; the lab rubric grades the justification structure, not the labels. Write your mapping before opening the discussion.

  • Step 1. Collect the week's advisories.
  • Step 2. Deduplicate and normalize them.
  • Step 3. Summarize each for the SOC.
  • Step 4. Extract indicators into the blocklist pipeline.
  • Step 5. Assess which advisories affect your organization, using the asset inventory.
  • Step 6. Draft the weekly executive briefing.
  • Step 7. Decide whether to invoke emergency patching for one item.
  • Step 8. Brief the CIO, including a TLP:AMBER report received from your sector's sharing community.

Scenario: compare your boundary mapping

Committed to your eight labels and justifications? Open the discussion of the expected answers and defensible disagreements.

  • Steps 1 and 2: AI delegable (with spot checks). Collection retrieval and processing are text movement; the asymmetry we identified in the intelligence cycle. Note that collection priorities would be direction, and human.
  • Steps 3, 4, 6: AI drafted with human verification is the expected center of gravity, and pure positions are defensible if argued: step 4's output flows into enforcement (the silent failure argument from extraction pushes toward mandatory verification), and step 6's executive register smooths uncertainty (as we saw in summarization), so the human pass is about restoring caveats, not just catching errors.
  • Step 5: human only, forced by access. The judgment requires the asset inventory and internal context. If your organization has connected that data to a local model, the answer can shift toward AI assisted, which is exactly why the justification matters more than the label.
  • Step 7: human only, forced by irreversibility plus accountability. Emergency patching spends real organizational resources and risk. The model can propose; the organization decides.
  • Step 8: human only on two grounds. Accountability for the CIO conversation, and a TLP constraint: the TLP:AMBER report may not transit any external AI service. A local model summarization may be permissible under the marking; sending it to an external API is not. If you caught the TLP trap, your governance instincts are working.

Close the loop

At the start of this module you made delegation calls on six work items using only intuition. Return to those notes now with the four properties and TLP in hand. Which calls would you change, and which property changed your mind? Record two or three sentences in your course notes before moving to the lab.

Knowledge Check 2

A 2024 era model recommends mitigating a vulnerability by upgrading to version 4.2 of a product. The vendor's current advisory recommends 4.6 and notes that 4.2 contained an incomplete fix. The model's claim is best described as:

Use the bookmarks from earlier (NVD, KEV, cve.org, FIRST CVSS) as your routing targets for the lab. Read the FIRST TLP 2.0 specification before starting it.

Analysis of competing hypotheses (ACH). Heuer's structured analytic technique for attribution class problems: enumerate hypotheses, score evidence against all of them, and favor the hypothesis with the least disconfirming evidence rather than the most confirming. The disciplined antidote to settling on the first plausible story.

Scaling verification. At feed scale we cannot hand verify everything; organizations layer automated validation, sampling, and human review by stakes. Designing that layering is a Module 11 and 12 conversation; this module gives us the unit operation.

Sharing communities. ISACs and similar sector-based sharing organizations are where TLP governs daily practice. Their handling rules are a window into how intelligence cooperation actually works between competitors.

Methodology limits. This methodology is tuned for vulnerability and advisory intelligence, where authoritative routing targets exist. For actor behavior and campaign reporting, ground truth is softer: we triangulate primary reports with stated commercial caveats, and confidence ceilings are lower.
