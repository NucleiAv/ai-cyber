Academic Papers & Preprints

  • Spracklen, J., et al. (2025). "We Have a Package for You! A Comprehensive Analysis of Package Hallucinations by Code Generating LLMs." USENIX Security Symposium. Paper
    Code-generating models recommend nonexistent packages at 5–22% rates; 58% of hallucinated package names recur across queries, enabling supply-chain attacks ("slopsquatting"). Cited in Section 3 as primary evidence that confident, structurally valid model output can fabricate specific identifiers, the same failure mode that produces fabricated CVE IDs in CTI workflows. Also cited in Module 2.
    USENIX Security '25
  • Evans, O. (2024). "Vintage Large Language Models." Talk transcript. Talk
    Proposes deliberately training models on data up to a chosen past date, which makes the knowledge-cutoff boundary explicit rather than incidental. Source of the supplementary video in Section 3, and the conceptual background for the frozen-model demonstration in Section 5.
    Transcript · Slides · Video
  • Alam, M., et al. (2024). "CTIBench: A Benchmark for Evaluating LLMs in Cyber Threat Intelligence Tasks." Paper
    Benchmark across CTI tasks including threat actor attribution, TTP extraction, and CVSS prediction from descriptions. Referenced in Section 3 optional enrichment as an example of CTI-specific evaluation methodology. Read with the course's standing benchmark caveats: datasets go stale and leaderboard performance is not deployment performance.
    arXiv:2406.07599

Frameworks & Standards

  • FIRST. Common Vulnerability Scoring System (CVSS) v3.1 Specification Document. Framework
    Metric definitions, base score formula, and severity bands for CVSS v3.1. Used in Section 2 for the annotated CVSS vector walk-through and the severity-vs-risk distinction. CVSS v4.0 is noted in optional enrichment; v3.1 remains the version in widest operational use at course time.
    first.org/cvss/specification-document
  • FIRST. Exploit Prediction Scoring System (EPSS). Framework
    Probabilistic scoring of the likelihood a vulnerability will be exploited in the next 30 days, combining CVE features with threat intelligence signals. Contrasted with CVSS severity in Section 2 to illustrate the severity-vs-risk distinction.
    first.org/epss
  • FIRST. Traffic Light Protocol (TLP) Version 2.0. Framework
    Four-color marking system governing how shared intelligence may be redistributed. Used in Section 5 to frame the compliance dimension of AI-assisted CTI workflows: TLP:AMBER content may not be sent to external AI services without organizational policy authorization.
    first.org/tlp
  • OASIS. STIX Version 2.1. Standard
    Structured Threat Information eXpression: the machine-readable CTI format. Defines STIX Domain Objects (SDOs), STIX Relationship Objects (SROs), the STIX patterning language for indicators, and bundle structure. Section 4 uses STIX 2.1 for the annotated IoC indicator example.
    docs.oasis-open.org/cti/stix/v2.1
  • OASIS. TAXII Version 2.1. Standard
    Trusted Automated eXchange of Intelligence Information: the transport protocol for STIX bundles. Defines the Collection and Channel models. Section 4 introduces TAXII as the distribution layer for the STIX bundles produced by the extraction pipeline.
    docs.oasis-open.org/cti/taxii/v2.1
  • MITRE ATT&CK. Framework
    Adversary tactics, techniques, and procedures knowledge base. Named in Section 1 as the operational TTP vocabulary; full treatment is in Module 8. Mentioned here to document the forward reference.
    attack.mitre.org

Government Sources

  • NIST SP 800-150. Guide to Cyber Threat Information Sharing. Government
    Definitions of threat data, threat information, and cyber threat intelligence; indicator and TTP taxonomy; information sharing models. Used in Section 1 for core vocabulary and in Section 4 for sharing roles.
    csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/150/final
  • CVE Program. Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures. Government
    The CVE identifier system and the CNA authorization model. Authoritative for CVE existence and identity claims. Used in Sections 2 and 5.
    cve.org
  • NIST National Vulnerability Database (NVD). Government
    CVE enrichment with CVSS scores, CPE product identifiers, and CWE weakness classifications. Primary source for the Log4Shell (CVE-2021-44228) CVSS 10.0 vector and score used in Section 2 and the currency check in Section 5. Enrichment throughput and lag characteristics are discussed as a structural property.
    nvd.nist.gov
  • CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) Catalog. Government
    Confirmed actively exploited vulnerabilities, maintained by CISA. KEV inclusion is the authoritative source for exploitation status claims; KEV absence is not evidence of safety. Used in Sections 2 and 5. BOD 22-01 establishes the federal remediation mandate tied to KEV.
    cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog
  • ODNI Intelligence Community Directive 203 (ICD 203). Analytic Standards. Government
    Estimative language standards including confidence and likelihood bands used in finished intelligence products. Cited in Sections 3 and 5 as the source for the estimative language bands applied to CTI output.
    archive.dni.gov: ICD 203
  • Apache Software Foundation. Log4j Security Advisories. Advisory
    Fix version history for CVE-2021-44228 (Log4Shell): 2.15.0 was the initial fix, superseded by 2.16.0 and later releases as the initial fix was found incomplete. Used in Sections 2 and 5 as the canonical example of why currency matters; the vendor advisory is the primary source for product-specific version facts.
    logging.apache.org/security.html
  • NIST. NIST Updates NVD Operations to Address Record CVE Growth. April 2026. Government
    Announces the shift from universal NVD enrichment to a risk-based triage model. CVEs in the CISA KEV catalog are prioritized (one business day target), followed by federal government software and critical software under Executive Order 14028. All backlogged CVEs published before March 2026 are moved to "not scheduled." Used in Section 2 to illustrate why no single source is complete and why a multi-source verification map is essential.
    nist.gov: NVD Operations Update, April 2026
  • Executive Order 14028. Improving the Nation's Cybersecurity. May 2021. Government
    Defines "critical software" and requires federal agencies to apply enhanced security measures to it. Referenced in Section 2 as one of the three enrichment priority criteria in NIST's April 2026 NVD triage model.
    bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov: EO 14028

Practitioner & Institutional Resources

  • Heuer, R.J. Psychology of Intelligence Analysis. CIA Center for the Study of Intelligence, 1999. Available free. Practitioner
    The foundational text on cognitive bias in analytical work, including confirmation bias and the structured analytic techniques designed to counter it. Used in Section 5 for the analyst boundary argument: the four properties that place a decision in the human column (accountability, access, irreversibility, attribution) parallel the bias structures Heuer documents. The automation bias literature extends this into human-machine teaming contexts.
    cia.gov/resources/csi
  • US Joint Publication 2-0. Joint Intelligence. Doctrine
    The intelligence cycle model as used in national security doctrine. Section 1 uses this lineage descriptively, not prescriptively: the cycle is a shared vocabulary map, acknowledged to idealize a messier parallel reality. Critics within the intelligence community have raised this limitation; the course explicitly notes it.
    jcs.mil: 2-0 Intelligence Series
  • VulnCheck. NVD enrichment coverage analysis. 2026. Industry Analysis
    Analysis showing approximately 32% of CVE-2025 entries had been fully enriched by NVD as of early 2026, with roughly 10,000 vulnerabilities from 2025 still lacking a CVSS score. Cited in Section 2 to quantify the enrichment gap that motivated NIST's April 2026 triage shift.
    vulncheck.com
  • curl project maintainer public posts on AI-generated false vulnerability reports. Primary Source
    Documented publicly by the curl project's maintainers; cited in Section 3 as a real-world ecosystem cost of fluent fabrication, where AI-generated reports that look credible impose triage burden on open source maintainers.

Source Caveats

Reading Sources Critically

Every source has a context. When you read security research, keep these caveats in mind:

Source Category Key Caveat
Government vulnerability data (CVE, NVD, CISA KEV) Authoritative for what they record, and they do not record everything. Enrichment lags disclosure, and NVD moved away from universal enrichment in 2026, so an unscored record is an unenriched record. Absence from KEV is not evidence that a vulnerability is not being exploited.
Scoring frameworks (CVSS, EPSS) They measure different things and neither one is risk. CVSS describes intrinsic severity, EPSS estimates the probability of exploitation in the wild. Combining them requires your own environmental context.
Industry analysis (VulnCheck enrichment coverage) Single-vendor methodology. The coverage figure depends entirely on how that vendor defines an enriched record, so cite it with the definition attached.
Primary-source practitioner accounts (curl maintainer posts on AI-generated reports) First-hand and unmediated, but it is one project's experience reported by an interested party. Useful as an illustration, not as a base rate.
Talks and transcripts (Evans 2024) A talk has no methods section and no peer review. Cite the idea, not a measurement.
Benchmarks (CTIBench) Task-specific by construction. Performance on threat-intelligence benchmark tasks does not transfer to your reporting pipeline without your own evaluation.
Intelligence doctrine (ICD 203, Joint Publication 2-0, Heuer) Normative standards written for national-security practice on government timelines and authorities. Adapt the reasoning discipline; do not import the process wholesale into a commercial SOC.
