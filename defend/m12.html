AI governance, ethics and regulation

Who decides to deploy an AI system that no control fully protects, how to measure ethics instead of debating it, how to write a policy people can follow, and how to read regulations that keep changing.

The idea in one minute

Earlier modules showed that no technical control fully stops attacks on AI. So someone must decide what to deploy anyway, under what limits, and who owns the risk that remains. That decision is governance.

This module makes governance practical. Use the NIST AI RMF as an instrument, measure bias with the same confusion matrix as Module 1, write policy statements that are real controls, and assess one deployment against all four RMF functions.

Think of it like this

Governance is the house rules plus a named adult in charge. "Be careful" is a wish. "Nobody uses the stove without an adult in the room" is a rule someone can check.

Learn it from zero

Step 1. Residual risk needs an owner

After every control, some risk remains. If nobody is named to accept it, the decision is made by default. Section 1 calls this "the decision nobody assigned".

Step 2. The NIST AI RMF

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0 (2023) has four functions.

  • Govern. Policies, roles and accountability. It is the hub that touches the other three.
  • Map. Understand the context, the uses and the risks.
  • Measure. Test and track the risks with numbers.
  • Manage. Decide, act, and watch over time.

Step 3. Ethics you can measure

Bias shows up when you slice the confusion matrix by group. A model can have good overall accuracy and much higher false positive rates for one group. An aggregate number can hide this. Automation bias is the habit of trusting machine output too much, so "a human in the loop" is not a fix if the human just clicks yes.

Step 4. A policy is a control or a wish

A good AI use policy statement says who must do what, when, and how anyone can check it. "Analysts should use AI responsibly" is a wish. "Before an AI-drafted finding enters a ticket, the analyst attaches the source log line that supports it" is a control.

Step 5. Regulations

The EU AI Act sorts systems into risk tiers. Some practices are banned, high-risk systems carry strict duties, some systems have transparency duties, and the rest have few rules. Dates have moved, so read the current timeline, not a memory of it. ISO/IEC 42001 is a certifiable AI management system standard, built like ISO 27001.

Words you need

Residual risk
The risk that remains after controls.
AI RMF
NIST's AI Risk Management Framework, with Govern, Map, Measure and Manage.
Disparity
A difference in error rates between groups.
Automation bias
Trusting automated output too much, even when it is wrong.
Proportionality
A legal test. A measure that limits rights must be necessary and not excessive for its aim.
Gap assessment
Comparing what a deployment does against what a framework expects.
ISO/IEC 42001
An international standard for an AI management system.
