The triage pipeline. A. Parse extracts fields. B. Classify gives Critical, High, Medium, Low or Informational. C. Enrich adds context to Critical and High. D. Hand off passes alerts to analysts, most severe first.

Bench 1. Checking two parsers against ground truth

The log entry, in CEF.

CEF:0|AcmeFW|Firewall|2.1|401|Denied Connection|8|src=172.16.5.22 dst=10.10.1.50 dpt=22 proto=TCP spt=51442 act=deny cs1Label=Reason cs1=Policy violation

The header has seven fields split by |. Field 7 is the CEF severity, 0 to 10, in bands. 0 to 3 Low, 4 to 6 Medium, 7 to 8 High, 9 to 10 Very High. A field that is not in the entry has ground truth NOT PRESENT.

Bench 2. Naming the failure

Key terms. Hallucinated IOC, severity inflation, severity deflation, context fabrication, knowledge cutoff.

Bench 3. One Suricata alert, read field by field

<38>1 2026-05-15T03:22:17Z idsensor01 suricata 5501 - - [1:2024897:3] ET MALWARE Win32/Agent.UNK CnC Beacon [Priority: 1] {TCP} 192.168.4.100:49152 -> 203.0.113.45:443

The syslog envelope is <38>, version, timestamp and sensor name. The Suricata message is the signature ID, the signature name, the rule author's priority (1 is highest), the protocol, and source to destination. 192.168.x.x is a private range, used only inside an organization.

Bench 4. One alert, five runs

Over five runs the LLM classifier said High, Medium, High, High and Medium. The SIEM rule said High all five times. The pipeline escalates only High.

Case A. A beacon triaged as Low

DNS queries from an internal host every 5 to 7 minutes all day. The domain was registered 48 hours before. The C2 framework is nine months newer than the model's training data. The LLM said Low, routine scan, likely a vulnerability assessment tool. The compromise was found 12 hours later by other means.

Bench 5. What the score costs your team

Vendor quote. Precision 0.40, recall 0.90. 200 actionable alerts reach triage each week. Recall = TP ÷ (TP + FN). Precision = TP ÷ (TP + FP).

Bench 6. Choosing the operating point

This SOC sees 10,000 events a day. 3 are real attacks. The team can investigate 15 alerts a day. Detectors A (random forest, AUC 0.97, TPR 0.85 at FPR 0.001, 0.98 at 0.01), B (isolation forest, AUC 0.82, 0.45 and 0.74) and C (logistic regression, AUC 0.88, 0.60 and 0.83).

Case B. A critical alert triaged as Low

02:14 the ML layer raises a critical alert. Unusual SMB traffic from a radiology workstation to the domain controller. 02:14 the LLM enrichment step calls it Low, routine network scan. 02:15 the overnight analyst reads Low, accepts it without opening the evidence, and moves on. 14:00 ransomware encrypts patient records, imaging and billing.

Before you adopt. Questions for a vendor

Four analysts. About 8,000 events a day reach the queue, and signature rules already close most of them. A vendor offers an LLM triage assistant quoting precision 0.72 and recall 0.88. Nobody has piloted it.

Find it, name it, fix it

Prompt clinic

Field card. Keep this

  • Check every machine output against the source line. Absent fields are NOT PRESENT.
  • Severity deflation is the failure that hides attacks.
  • One LLM run is a sample. Rules are deterministic.
  • Work out investigations per analyst per day before you buy.
  • Compare detectors at the false positive rate you can afford, not by AUC.
  • Flag any disagreement between detection and triage.
